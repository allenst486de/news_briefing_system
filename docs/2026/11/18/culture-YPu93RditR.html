‘His wife was horrified’: did Edward Hopper paint his sexual problems?

He is the maestro of American melancholy. But in a new biography, the artist is called a ‘sadist’ and a ‘prize hog’ by his wife – who was aghast to see their domestic and bedroom problems surface in his most famous worksIt should have been a happy occasion. In 1950, an Edward Hopper retrospective was staged at the Whitney Museum of American Art in New York – but the artist’s wife, the painter Josephine (Jo) Nivison Hopper, was in a fury. The show’s catalogue, written by the museum’s director, had originally stated that in 1924 Edward “married the artist Josephine Nivison Hopper”, yet in the final version, the phrase “the artist” had been removed. “I’m not allowed to exist even in my husband’s biography,” Jo seethed in a private notebook that has just come to light. “And that’s all right with EH.”Due to her own strenuous efforts, Jo not only exists, but is a vital protagonist in my new biography of Edward Hopper. Jo once contemplated writing her husband’s biography herself, having amassed such an overwhelming body of material documenting their tumultuous marriage. As Edward’s creative partner, muse, curator, producer and unofficial manager, Jo produced 35 years’ worth of daily diaries, almost 100 notebooks, reels of letters, and four ledger books documenting Edward’s works.
