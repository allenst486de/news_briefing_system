Russell T Davies, David Hare and more give their takes“James Bond will return,” says the closing title on No Time to Die, the most recent James Bond film and the final one to star Daniel Craig. It is just as well: Bond has to all appearances been vaporised by sea-launched missiles, having been infected by nanobots programmed to attack his nearest and dearest at the slightest touch. No one would believe that one of the world’s most successful film series would come to such an abrupt halt, but no doubt a little reassurance helped.Nevertheless, the first order of business for scriptwriter Steven Knight and director Denis Villeneuve will be dealing with it: how to revive and re-energise a character at the heart of the action. Speculation on their best way forward has been rife, and previous examples are not hard to find. Sherlock Holmes was killed off by Arthur Conan Doyle by fighting his arch enemy Professor Moriarty at the Reichenbach Falls in the story The Final Problem, only to resuscitate him a decade later with The Adventure of the Empty House. Superman is apparently killed at the end of Batman v Superman: Dawn of Justice, but resurrected in Justice League for the fight against Steppenwolf. Arguably the most enduring pop-culture return is Bobby Ewing’s famous shower scene in the TV show Dallas in 1986, which attempted to ensure continuity by suggesting Ewing’s death was his wife’s dream. And even Ian Fleming toyed with killing off Bond, with a climactic poison scene at the end of his 1957 novel From Russia with Love, and leaving him amnesiac and vulnerable to Soviet attack at the finish of You Only Live Twice, the last Bond novel published in Fleming’s lifetime.
