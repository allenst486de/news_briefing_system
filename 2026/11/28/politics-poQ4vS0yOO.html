‘Andy time’ may frustrate the media and voters impatient for change – but after years of short premierships and rushed policies, isn’t this the reset Westminster needs?Andy Burnham is making a lot of people impatient. Political journalists, pro-EU activists, climate campaigners, fossil-fuel lobbyists, recipients of welfare benefits, advocates of welfare cuts, enemies of neoliberalism, enthusiasts for renationalisation, members of the armed forces and even cabinet colleagues: all are waiting for the prime minister to make big decisions, and thus reveal the true nature of his administration, four months after its beginning.Since his Manchester mayoralty, some of Burnham’s co-workers have had a phrase for his tendency to take things at his own tempo: “Andy time”. He lets interesting conversations overrun, likes to ponder issues without being rushed, and pushes back crucial announcements. The unveiling of his 10-year plan – a typically ambitious but loose Burnham timespan, and one of his government’s pivotal moments – has been repeatedly delayed.
