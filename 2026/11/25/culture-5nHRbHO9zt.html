The results are baffling, unengaging and soulless‘An astonishing sight’: Oliver Wainwright reviews the building’s architectureThe Museum of London at the Barbican was the capital’s local museum, a crowded and quirky presentation of its history from the Dagenham idol to Vauxhall Pleasure Gardens. But apparently the location deterred casual visitors, and maybe it just wasn’t grand enough to reflect London’s self-conscious significance. For not since ancient Rome has a capital dominated its nation’s history and culture like London. So now we get the rebranded and reimagined London Museum Smithfield.History surrounds you in this neighbourhood: Black Death burials by the Charterhouse, the Knights of St John, the Smithfield martyrs, William Wallace’s execution site. So goodbye scruffy brutalist Slough House location, hello deep history. Except when you enter the new museum, all that history, all those layers going back to the Romans and earlier, have been brushed away. If you want an illustration that bigger is not better, this is it. When Sharon Ament, the director, asked me if I liked it, I couldn’t find the words. I was heartbroken, speechless, unlike the museum’s management who have their own way with language. Displays have nonsensical themes: “London as Ritual”, “London as Stage”, “London as Sunrise”.
